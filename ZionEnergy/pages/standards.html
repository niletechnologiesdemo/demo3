Precedence — which rule wins
When two criteria sets could apply to the same item, the platform resolves in this order: customer contract spec → OEM spec → governing industry standard (DS-1, API, AWS) → Zion Spec. The resolved set is stamped onto the inspection at dispatch, so a report can always be defended by pointing at the exact revision that was in force on the day the work was done — even after the standard is superseded.

Equipment gating
Every method names the equipment and the reference standard it must be run against. At dispatch the platform checks the assigned crew’s kit: if the ET set’s calibration has lapsed, or the notched reference tube is not on the truck, the job cannot be marked ready. The same check blocks an inspector without the required certification from being assigned in the first place.

Customer & OEM overrides
Overrides are attached to the customer record, not typed into the report. NOV’s 0.180 in minimum wall on thru tubing motors travels with every NOV job automatically. When the customer changes the spec, one edit updates every future inspection — and the historic reports keep the revision they were run under.

This mapping is what makes the chain automatic
This one table is the difference between a form and a system. The customer picks an asset on the request; everything downstream — standard, procedure, method, technique, equipment, acceptance criteria, inspector qualification and the report format that gets issued — is resolved from this row. Nobody chooses it on site, so nobody can choose it wrong.

Fractions in, decimals out
The crew types 6 7/16 because that is what the tape reads. The tolerance band is 6.375 – 6.438. The platform normalises the fraction, compares it against the band for that exact connection, and returns the verdict on the spot — no mental arithmetic on a rig floor at 2am, and no out-of-tolerance dimension quietly passing because someone rounded the wrong way.
